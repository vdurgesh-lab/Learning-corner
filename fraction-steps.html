Fraction Steps

Learn fractions one small step at a time: equal parts, sharing, the number line, then adding and subtracting.

34

Top number counts the parts we have. It matches the shaded pieces.

Bottom number tells how many equal parts make one whole.

For teachers: why it is built this way

Every lesson runs the same four parts, so students spend their attention on fractions, not on figuring out the screen.

  • Quick rememberTwo questions from earlier lessons, answered from memory before anything new.
  • WatchWorked examples, one step at a time, ending with a "Say it" sentence students read aloud.
  • Your turn12 problems of several different kinds per lesson, and a fresh set any time. No question repeats. After a right answer, a picture shows why. After a wrong one, a picture shows where it went wrong, and a similar problem comes back later.
  • RememberOne rule card with a picture and a sentence to say. All the cards come back together in the mixed review.

Using it in class

  • Keep sessions to about 15 to 20 minutes. One lesson part at a time is fine.
  • Before the screen, fold and cut paper strips, and share real or paper "pizzas". Then the pictures here mean something real.
  • Have students read every "Say it" sentence aloud, in full. Say fractions as words ("three fourths"), not "three over four".
  • Come back to Quick remember and Lesson 9 on later days. Spaced practice is what makes it stick.
